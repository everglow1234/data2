## 1. What is an Index in Research?

### Definition

An **index** is a composite statistic that aggregates multiple indicators to measure changes in a representative group of individual data points. In simpler terms, it's a single number created by combining several different measurements to represent a complex concept that cannot be captured by just one indicator.

### Purpose and Importance

Indexes are used to summarize some more general concept by combining several indicators. They serve multiple critical functions:

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Simplification** | They make complex, multidimensional concepts easier to understand and communicate |
| **Comparison** | They enable ranking and comparison across countries, organizations, or time periods |
| **Policy Making** | They provide evidence-based metrics for decision-makers |
| **Progress Tracking** | They help monitor changes over time in specific domains |

### How Indexes Differ from Individual Indicators

On their own, none of the individual questions is likely to provide as accurate a representation as the collection of all questions together. For example, measuring financial security requires considering income, savings, debt, and emergency funds together rather than just one factor.

### Index vs. Scale

Indexes and scales differ in two ways:

1. **Items that comprise an index are conceptually different from one another**, whereas component items in a scale are conceptually similar
2. **Scale items have an intensity structure** showing greater intensity of experience or belief

## 2. Gender Indexes: Major Measures of Gender Equality and Gender Gap

### 2.1 Global Gender Gap Index (GGGI)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Organization** | World Economic Forum |
| **Purpose** | The index is designed to measure gender-based gaps in access to resources and opportunities in countries rather than the actual level of available resources and opportunities |
| **Scale** | Ranges from 0.000 (0%, lowest possible gender equality) to 1.000 (100%, highest possible gender equality) |

#### Dimensions Measured:
- Economic Participation and Opportunity
- Educational Attainment
- Health and Survival
- Political Empowerment

#### Current Status:
- According to the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report 2023, it will take **131 years** for the gender gap to close
- **Top Performers:** Iceland retained its number one spot for the 14th year in a row, being also the only country with a percentage point above 90%

---

### 2.2 Gender Inequality Index (GII)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Organization** | United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) |
| **Introduction** | Launched in 2010 in the Human Development Report |
| **Purpose** | Reflects inequality in achievements between women and men in three dimensions: reproductive health, empowerment and the labour market |
| **Scale** | Ranges between 0 and 1, with 0 being 0% inequality (women fare equally) and 1 being 100% inequality (women fare poorly) |

#### Components:

**Reproductive Health**
- Maternal mortality ratio
- Adolescent fertility rate

**Empowerment**
- Share of parliamentary seats held by each gender
- Secondary and higher education attainment levels

**Labor Market**
- Women's participation in the workforce

---

### 2.3 Gender Equality Index (EU)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Organization** | European Institute for Gender Equality (EIGE) |
| **Purpose** | A tool to measure the progress of gender equality in several areas of economic and social life in the EU and its Member States |
| **Scale** | The Index consists of 31 indicators and ranges from 1 to 100, with 100 representing a gender-equal society |
| **Current Status** | In 2022, the Gender Equality Index score for the EU is 68.6 points out of 100, only 5.5 points higher than in 2010 |

#### Domains Measured:

| Domain | Focus Areas |
|--------|-------------|
| **Work** | Labor market participation, segregation, quality of work |
| **Money** | Earnings, income, poverty risk |
| **Knowledge** | Educational attainment and segregation |
| **Time** | Care activities and social activities |
| **Power** | Political, economic, and social decision-making |
| **Health** | Status, behaviors, and access to services |

---

### 2.4 Other Gender Measures

| Index | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Gender Development Index (GDI)** | Introduced in 1995 alongside GEM, focuses on gender-sensitive dimensions of the Human Development Index |
| **Gender Empowerment Measure (GEM)** | Measures whether women and men are able to actively participate in economic and political life and take part in decision-making |
| **SDG Gender Index** | The most comprehensive global measure of gender equality, benchmarking gender equality across 139 countries and 56 issues across 14 of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals |

## 3. Why We Need Gender Indexes

### 3.1 Policy Development and Implementation

The GII is an indispensable tool for shaping gender-sensitive policies at the national and international levels. By providing a clear picture of gender disparities, the GII allows policymakers to target specific areas where interventions are needed.

**Specific Applications:**
- Countries with high GII scores due to poor reproductive health can prioritize maternal healthcare services
- Countries with low female political representation can develop initiatives to increase women's participation in decision-making
- Resource allocation can be directed to areas with the greatest gender gaps

### 3.2 Awareness and Advocacy

Beyond policy-making, the GII also plays an essential role in raising awareness about gender inequality on a global scale. It brings attention to countries and regions where gender disparities are most pronounced, fostering international dialogue and cooperation.

### 3.3 Progress Monitoring and Accountability

By tracking the progress (or lack thereof) in closing gender gaps, the index serves as a tool for ongoing accountability and improvement. Gender indexes enable:

- ✅ Benchmarking performance across countries
- ✅ Tracking changes over time
- ✅ Holding governments accountable to commitments
- ✅ Evaluating effectiveness of gender equality programs

### 3.4 Economic and Development Rationale

Sustainable development goals and other economic targets are often unachievable if half of a country's population is hampered by restricted opportunities. Gender equality indexes help demonstrate:

- The economic cost of gender inequality
- Lost human development potential
- Barriers to inclusive growth
- Areas where investment in gender equality yields highest returns

### 3.5 International Comparisons and Best Practices

Gender indexes enable countries to:

- Learn from high-performing nations
- Identify effective policies and interventions
- Understand cultural and structural factors affecting equality
- Set realistic targets based on comparable contexts

## 5. How Organizations Create Indexes: General Methodology

### The OECD 10-Step Framework

The OECD and European Commission's Joint Research Centre published a handbook describing ten recursive steps for developing an index:

---

### Step 1: Develop Conceptual Framework

Before deciding which variables should be used in the index, it is important to work with stakeholders to clarify the purpose of the index and define the analysis question.

**Key Questions:**
- What phenomenon are we measuring?
- What are the key dimensions?
- Who is the target audience?
- How will the index be used?

---

### Step 2: Select Data and Indicators

Items should be selected based on their content validity, unidimensionality, the degree of specificity in which a dimension is to be measured, and their amount of variance.

**Selection Criteria:**
- Analytical soundness
- Measurability and data availability
- Country/entity coverage
- Relevance to the phenomenon
- Relationship between indicators

---

### Step 3: Handle Missing Data

Preparation of data and imputation of missing data, in order to provide a complete dataset.

**Common Approaches:**
- List-wise deletion (removing incomplete cases)
- Mean/median substitution
- Multiple imputation
- Expert judgment

---

### Step 4: Conduct Multivariate Analysis

Items should be empirically related to one another, examining their multivariate relationships.

**Techniques:**
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
- Factor Analysis
- Correlation Analysis
- Cluster Analysis

---

### Step 5: Normalize Data

Normalisation of data, to render the variables comparable.

**Common Methods:**
- Min-Max normalization (scales between 0-1 or 0-100)
- Standardization (z-scores)
- Ranking
- Distance to reference

---

### Step 6: Weight and Aggregate

Weighting and aggregation, along the lines of the underlying theoretical framework.

#### Weighting Approaches:

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Equal weights** | All indicators contribute equally |
| **Statistical weights** | Based on variance or factor loadings |
| **Expert judgment** | Determined by stakeholder consultation |
| **Public participation** | Through surveys or consultations |

#### Aggregation Methods:

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Arithmetic mean** | Simple average (fully compensatory) |
| **Geometric mean** | Can be used to combine measurements from scales with different distributional properties and eliminates the need for standardization before combining them |
| **Weighted sum** | Linear combination with weights |
| **Multi-criteria methods** | Non-compensatory approaches |

---

### Step 7: Assess Uncertainty and Sensitivity

Test how robust the index is to:
- Different weighting schemes
- Alternative normalization methods
- Missing data treatment
- Indicator selection

---

### Step 8: Link to Other Variables

Validate the index by examining:
- Correlation with related concepts
- Predictive validity
- Face validity with experts
- Construct validity

---

### Step 9: Visualize and Present Results

The Calculate Composite Index tool guides you through the process of building an appropriate index and helps you visualize and understand the results.

**Presentation Formats:**
- Rankings and league tables
- Maps and geographical visualizations
- Dashboards showing component indicators
- Time series showing trends
- Scatter plots showing relationships

---

### Step 10: Document and Communicate

Be transparent about methods, assumptions and limitations. The modeller's objectives must be clearly stated at the outset, and the chosen model must be tested to see to what extent it fulfils the modeller's goal.

---

### Key Characteristics of Good Indexes

> **Characteristics of an Index:**
> - **Robust** – Not thrown off by random or partial variations
> - **Discriminating** – Able to distinguish between different cases
> - **Efficient** – Reasonably easy to build and measure
> - **Effective** – Captures what we want to measure

## 7. The Equiverse Index: TalentNomics India's Proposed Sector-Gender-Role Index

### Project Title: Mapping the Leadership Funnel: A Visual Index of Gender-Role Parity

**Focus:** Visualizing the "Leaky Pipeline" through Comparative Gender-Role Pyramids

TalentNomics India has developed a strategic proposal for a groundbreaking index that would measure gender representation across hierarchical levels within Indian organizations, sector by sector. This proposal represents a significant evolution from traditional gender indexes.

---

### 7.1 Core Hypothesis and Context

#### The Vision

The Equiverse Index is rooted in TalentNomics India's vision to restore an "Equiverse"—a universe where gender equity is the norm. The index operates on a powerful hypothesis:

> **If we bring about gender parity at leadership levels, it will have a cascading effect and a more gender equal world will follow.**

#### The Shifting Challenge

The fundamental problem has evolved. It's no longer primarily about enrollment of girls in schools or the percentage of women graduates. The core challenge has shifted to:

- A comparatively low percentage of women entering the job market
- An even lower percentage staying and rising to leadership roles

#### The Current Crisis

According to the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report 2025, it will still take **over 123 years** to reach full parity at the current rate of progress. Simply "getting women into the room is not enough"—we must ensure they can ascend through it.

#### The Leaky Pipeline and Broken Rung

The landscape is characterized by two critical phenomena:

| Phenomenon | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **The Leaky Pipeline** | Women enter the workforce in high numbers but disappear at every successive management level |
| **The Broken Rung** | At the first critical step up to manager, women are far less likely to get promoted. For every 100 men promoted to manager in 2018, 79 women were promoted, and in 2024, just 81 women were |

#### Why Current "Diversity" Reporting Fails

Without disaggregated, role-specific data, the diversity reported by corporations often masks a lack of women in decision-making roles and women's participation in the future of work.

---

### 7.2 Research Objective

To develop a standardized, annually or biennially updated **Sectoral Gender-Role Pyramid Index** for listed companies in key identified industries that:

1. ✅ Visualizes the distribution of men vs. women across 5 hierarchical tiers (Entry, Junior, Middle, Senior, C-Suite), with Board as an add-on
2. ✅ Identifies specific "bottleneck tiers" where gender attrition is acute in different sectors
3. ✅ Identifies best-in-class performers within sectors to indicate possibilities
4. ✅ Learns from best practices across sectors
5. ✅ Becomes a reference point for industries over time to measure improvement

> **Critical Note:** The goal is not 100% accuracy but to maximize the reliability of the index as a directional and comparative tool.

---

### 7.3 Strategic Rationale: Why Visualization Matters

The Equiverse Index is not merely a social audit; it is a diagnostic tool for economic and organizational resilience. Three compelling reasons underscore its importance:

#### I. The "Financial Alpha" of Diverse Leadership

Gender parity is a proven driver of outperformance. Recent market data reveals that **female-led companies in the S&P 500 have outperformed their male-led peers by over 120% in total returns over the last decade.**

Diverse leadership teams are documented to:
- Process information more carefully
- Reduce "groupthink"
- Be significantly less likely to be embroiled in corporate controversies such as fraud or bribery

#### II. Mitigating "Retention Debt" and Attrition Costs

Replacing a senior leader is not just a talent loss; it is a financial drain. Research suggests that **replacing a C-suite executive can cost up to 200% of their annual salary** in recruitment, training, and lost institutional knowledge.

In 2024, women's retention scores dropped nearly **four times faster** than men's.

The Index aims to identify exactly where this "retention bleed" occurs, allowing organizations to fix the leak before it becomes a multi-million-dollar liability.

#### III. The "AI Integrity Gap" & Future-Proofing

As corporations integrate Generative AI into their core operations, a new "digital divide" is emerging:

- Only **21 percent** of entry-level women are encouraged by their managers to use AI tools
- **33 percent** of men at their level receive such encouragement
- Men are currently being encouraged to use and lead AI initiatives at a higher rate than women

If the decision-makers overseeing AI adoption are not diverse, the resulting algorithms will embed historical biases. The Equiverse Index will monitor the impact of AI at different levels for different roles and be an early warning for sectors to take corrective actions to ensure women are not disproportionately impacted.

> **It ensures that the "Architects of the Future" are as diverse as the populations they serve.**

### 7.5 Methodology and Data Collection

The Equiverse Index employs a **four-phase approach** combining traditional research methods with innovative data sourcing:

---

#### Phase 1: Data Mining from Public Domain

**Primary Source:** SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR)

Since FY 2022-23, SEBI mandates that the top 1,000 listed Indian companies prepare BRSR disclosures under the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015. This provides unprecedented access to gender-disaggregated workforce data.

**Key BRSR Gender Disclosures Include:**
- Gender diversity of employees across levels
- Turnover rates by gender
- Median wages by gender
- Gender composition of board and management
- Measures for differently-abled employees by gender
- Training and development by gender

**BRSR Core (Introduced July 2023):** The BRSR Core consists of 9 key ESG parameters including "enabling gender diversity in business." The top 250 listed companies now require third-party assessment of these disclosures, enhancing data reliability.

**Data Extraction Strategy:**
1. Systematically extract gender-segregated workforce data from BRSR reports for top 500 listed Indian companies
2. Focus on companies within the five target sectors
3. Create standardized database organizing data by company, sector, and hierarchical level
4. Track year-over-year changes

> **Current Advantage:** As of FY 2024-25, industry standards for BRSR Core reporting have been established, improving data comparability and quality.

---

#### Phase 2: Unorthodox Triangulation

For roles where formal reporting is weak or for non-listed companies that may be included for sectoral completeness:

**LinkedIn Data Scraping:**
- Use LinkedIn's public profiles to gather data on employee distribution by level and gender
- Particularly valuable for professional services, consulting, and technology roles
- Can provide insights into career progression patterns

**Name-Gender API Processing:**
- Deploy gender inference algorithms (e.g., Genderize.io, Gender API) to estimate gender distribution where not explicitly disclosed
- Acknowledge limitations and confidence intervals in reporting
- Cross-validate with available BRSR data where overlap exists

**Triangulation Approach:** Combine three data sources for maximum reliability:

| Source | Reliability Level |
|--------|-------------------|
| Official BRSR disclosures | Most reliable |
| LinkedIn organizational data | Moderately reliable |
| Name-gender inference | Least reliable, used to fill gaps only |

Assign confidence scores to each data point based on source reliability.

---

#### Phase 3: Visualization - The "Equiverse Pyramids"

**Creating Sector-Specific Gender-Role Pyramids:**

Transform numerical data into intuitive visual representations showing:

**Vertical Axis: Five hierarchical tiers**
- Tier 1: Entry Level
- Tier 2: Junior Management
- Tier 3: Middle Management
- Tier 4: Senior Management
- Tier 5: C-Suite
- Board* (as add-on overlay)

**Horizontal Axis: Percentage representation split by gender**
- Left side: Men
- Right side: Women
- Visual narrowing shows where the "leak" occurs

**Pyramid Interpretation:**

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Ideal "Equiverse" Pyramid** | Symmetrical at all levels |
| **Typical Reality** | Wide base (near parity at entry), dramatic narrowing at middle/senior levels |
| **Sector Comparisons** | Side-by-side pyramids reveal which sectors have healthier pipelines |

**Visual Features:**
- Color coding by gender
- Highlighting of critical "bottleneck tiers"
- Overlay of industry average
- Year-over-year animation showing progress/regression
- "Leak points" marked where drop-off exceeds 10 percentage points between levels

---

#### Phase 4: The "Equity Score"

**Calculating a Numerical Index:**

Assign each sector and company a numerical equity score based on:

**Area Under the Curve (AUC) Methodology:**
1. Calculate the area representing female representation across the top three tiers (Middle, Senior, C-Suite)
2. Compare to the theoretical maximum (50% representation at all levels)
3. Score = (Actual AUC / Maximum AUC) × 100

**Weighted Equity Score:** Alternatively, assign different weights to different tiers:

| Tier | Weight |
|------|--------|
| Entry Level | 10% |
| Junior Management | 15% |
| Middle Management | 25% |
| Senior Management | 30% |
| C-Suite | 20% |

This weighting reflects that leadership parity is more critical than entry-level parity for systemic change.

**Sector Benchmarking:**
- Establish sector-specific baseline scores
- Identify top quartile performers (best-in-class)
- Track improvement over time

**Company-Level Equity Score:** Individual organizations receive:
- Overall equity score (0-100)
- Tier-by-tier breakdown
- Comparison to sector average
- Identification of specific "broken rungs"
- Recommendations for improvement

### 7.12 Limitations and Transparency

#### Acknowledged Limitations:

| Limitation | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Data Completeness** | Not all companies report with equal rigor; some gaps will require inference |
| **Role Classification** | Companies may categorize roles differently; standardization is imperfect |
| **Gender Binary** | BRSR reporting currently focuses on male/female; non-binary inclusion is limited |
| **Causality** | The index measures representation, not the causal factors behind gaps |
| **Intersectionality** | While critical, granular intersectional data (race, caste, disability) is less available |
| **Private Companies** | Focus on listed companies means large private firms and startups are excluded |
| **Inference Accuracy** | Name-gender APIs have known limitations and biases |

---

#### Commitment to Transparency:
- Full methodology documentation published
- Data sources and confidence levels disclosed for each metric
- Limitations prominently stated in all reports
- Willingness to revise methodology based on feedback
- Open invitation for academic scrutiny and replication

---

#### Continuous Improvement Philosophy

The goal is not 100% accuracy but maximum reliability. The index will evolve as:
- BRSR reporting improves
- Better data sources emerge
- Stakeholder feedback is incorporated
- Research on gender metrics advances

> As stated in the proposal: **"We can't make 100% accuracy our goal; our goal is to maximize the reliability of the index"** as a directional and comparative tool that drives action.
